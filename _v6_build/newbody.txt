Only 85,739 customers, 5.7 per cent of accounts, used any DNSE product in December 2025, and only 27,100, or 1.8 per cent, held net assets of VND 10 million or more (DNSE, 2026d).

1.3 Business area and question
This report asks how DNSE can turn its account base into funded relationships and which savers to target first: where between sign-up and profit value is lost against the market and peers, why, and which response creates most value within DNSE's capabilities, funding and regulation. Investment banking, proprietary trading and new ventures are out of scope.
@@@
3.1 Why the money does not stay
Figure 5 traces the balance gap to five causes, each marked as measured or hypothesis (Tables B1, B2 and B2a). Three are measured and within DNSE's control.
The cash product gives savers no reason to stay. DNSE's idle-cash account pays a typical customer 2.1 to 2.3 per cent, against 6.0 per cent at TCBS's iPower for the same balance and 3.5 to 4.3 per cent on banks' automatic-earning accounts (DNSE, 2026b; TCBS, 2026a; Figure B1).
Nothing turns income into regular funding. DNSE offers no recurring investment or savings plan, so every deposit is a new decision (DNSE, 2026e). Yet 77 per cent of investors find managing their own assets difficult, and 36 per cent know a digital wealth service but only 16 per cent have used one (Nhân Dân, 2025; VnEconomy, 2025).
Trust breaks at the first deposit and at exit. Bonus terms appear in 5.6 per cent of DNSE's reviews against 0.7 per cent at VPS, and trust complaints draw 43 per cent of helpful votes on negative reviews (Section 2.3).
Two causes lie outside a savings product: derivatives build share and brokerage losses but no balances, because margin is posted at the clearing house; and targets may reward accounts rather than balances, a hypothesis for Round 3.
Alternative explanations fail: every index rose in 2025 while assets per account fell, DNSE's 2026 cash outflow matched the industry (Figure B2), capital is not binding and peers control for dormant accounts. Only "acquire young, monetise later" needs cohort data, the first Round 3 test.

3.2 Market gap and customers
Conditions in 2026 raise the cost of the gap (Table B3). Banks raised 12-month deposit rates by about 2.5 points, household deposits passed VND 11 quadrillion, cash at brokers fell by more than a third in three quarters, and bank-owned brokers supplied more than half of the second quarter's growth in margin lending (VietnamNet, 2026; Vietstock, 2026g, 2026i; VnEconomy, 2026b). One tenth of one per cent of household deposits equals 5.6 times DNSE's investor cash (Table B4).
The market gap lies between three kinds of provider (Table B5). Banks hold salaries and trust but offer little investment choice, fund apps offer monthly plans but no trading or lending, and brokers offer trading and lending but no path from a salary.
Findex data show that 37 per cent of adults receive wages into an account and that working adults in the upper income groups hold the largest formally saved surplus (World Bank, 2025b; Figure 6). DNSE's base is young: Gen Z held 31.5 per cent of its accounts in mid-2025 (VnExpress, 2025).

3.3 Lessons from abroad and from research
Zero-fee brokers abroad solved the same problem by gathering assets: Robinhood through net deposits of USD 68 billion in 2025, Trade Republic through automated savings plans (Table B6). Research explains why automation works: automatic enrolment raised pension-plan participation from 37 to 86 per cent (Madrian & Shea, 2001), and pre-committed saving raised saving rates from 3.5 to 13.6 per cent (Thaler & Benartzi, 2004). Securities firms cannot take deposits, so DNSE needs partners (Table B7).
@@@
3.4 Target market, priority problem and problem statement
Of five candidate markets, salaried mass-affluent adults rank first under both weightings: they hold the formally saved surplus, earn monthly income and are already in DNSE's base (Table B8). About 1.4 million DNSE accounts neither used a product nor held VND 10 million at the end of 2025; those of working customers are the first wave, already acquired and verified. Young first-time investors are DNSE's strongest franchise but weakest source of balances, so the scope is salaried customers of roughly 22 to 40, including working Gen Z, in their first 90 days or restarting a dormant account (Table B9).
Of seven candidate problems, the balance gap ranks first under both weightings on scale, urgency, strategic relevance, data support and control (Table B10). Loss-making brokerage, reliance on trading gains and funding cost are partly its consequences; the trust deficit ranks second and becomes a design condition. Meeting the 2026 profit plan from lending would need a loan book 2.3 times today's (Figure B3). The response therefore targets the three measured causes that DNSE controls; the brokerage loss needs a cost review, and the incentive hypothesis a Round 3 test.
Problem statement. DNSE opens nearly one in five of Vietnam's new securities accounts, yet it holds 2.3 to 3.5 per cent of investor cash and only 1.8 per cent of its accounts hold VND 10 million or more. Its products give salaried customers no competitive, automatic or trusted way to keep money on the platform, above all in the first 90 days. As a result, core profit has not grown since 2022 while revenue tripled (Table B11).

3.5 Strategic alternatives
Eight responses were scored on six weighted criteria, and Payday Portfolio ranks first under both weightings (Table B12). Two cheap options are necessary but insufficient: repricing idle cash costs about VND 20 billion a year per point on today's investor cash and creates no path for new money, and a trust programme alone creates no deposits. Both become parts of the preferred option. Acquisition-led growth adds the customers with the least money; a derivatives-to-shares bridge deepens exposure to the activity penalised in August 2026; an advisory tier needs advisers DNSE avoided; an alliance is a channel, not a strategy; and fees would break the zero-fee promise. Payday Portfolio sacrifices speed and cannot rescue the 2026 plan; a review of per-trade costs runs alongside.
@@@
4.1 The preliminary recommendation
DNSE should build Payday Portfolio, a savings tab in Entrade X, separate from trading, that turns a salaried customer's monthly surplus into an invested balance (Figure 7). It starts with the dormant accounts of working customers aged roughly 22 to 40, and each component answers a measured cause (Tables B13 and B14).
Salary link, for the missing path from income. The customer sets a goal and an amount, from VND 1 million, once. A standing order in the customer's own banking app, or a ZaloPay authorisation, moves the money on payday (Thanh Niên, 2023); DNSE never debits a bank account. Open banking interfaces, standard by March 2027, will make set-up a single step (State Bank of Vietnam, 2024).
Invested balance, for the uncompetitive cash rate. Cash passes through the idle-cash account and is invested by goal on the next trading day in Trứng Vàng bonds, VN30 exchange-traded funds and, once a distribution agreement is signed, partner bond funds. A market-linked cash yield through a partner bank or money market fund follows in phase 2, after SSC confirmation.
Trust layer, for the breaks at deposit and exit. No closure fee; pause, skip or cancel in one tap; rewards only on money that stays; all terms on one screen; and a report to the SSC before launch, once the control gaps found in August 2026 are closed (Market Times, 2026). Each promise has a published proof (Table B14a).
Margin is excluded at launch; collateral use is considered after 12 months, with a suitability check.

4.2 Value logic and expected outcomes
Value comes, in order of certainty, from keeping balances that would leave, distribution fees, cheaper growth from accounts already acquired and, in phase 2, lending at a 4.9-point spread. In the base case 150,000 savers, one in nine dormant accounts, contribute VND 1.5 million a month, a sixth of the average worker's VND 9.0 million (National Statistics Office, 2026), and keep 70 per cent. After 18 months the layer holds VND 2.8 trillion, 1.4 times DNSE's investor cash, earns about VND 23 billion a year in fees and lifts accounts holding VND 10 million or more from 1.8 to 10 per cent (Table B15; Figure 8). Fees of about VND 150,000 per saver a year mean a saver acquired for less pays back within a year.

4.3 Feasibility and risks
Phase 1 reuses what DNSE already runs: verified accounts, the idle-cash account, Trứng Vàng, exchange-traded fund trading, ZaloPay and Ensa as goal coach; the new build is a scheduling and goal engine (Table B16). Four risks each have a test. A bonus on all investor cash would cost VND 20 billion a year per point, so any bonus applies to new money and must pay for itself. The pilot needs no deposit-like structure. Inflows are tagged by source, so money moved from trading accounts is not counted as new. Payday transfers must reach 99.5 per cent success in shadow runs before launch.
@@@
4.4 Validation direction
Seven assumptions are tested before scale-up (Table B17; Figure B4). In months 3 to 6 a pilot randomises 20,000 dormant accounts of customers aged 22 to 40 into three arms: the current journey, Payday Portfolio, and Payday Portfolio with a bonus on new contributions, separating the effect of automation from that of yield. Thresholds follow the scenarios: enrolment of 11 per cent within 90 days, 70 per cent of contributions kept and 80 per cent new money support scale-up; below 4 per cent, the conservative case, the concept stops. The headline measure is one DNSE already reports: accounts holding VND 10 million or more, 1.8 per cent at the end of 2025, with a target of 5 per cent within 18 months.

5. Conclusion
DNSE has solved getting noticed, opening nearly one in five new securities accounts in Vietnam, but not getting customers to keep money with it. Only 1.8 per cent of its accounts hold VND 10 million or more, core profit has not grown since 2022, and each extra unit of revenue brings as much extra cost. Nothing turns a customer's income into money that stays. Payday Portfolio changes that with components DNSE already runs; Round 3 should test it in a three-arm pilot.
